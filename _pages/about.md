I am an M.Sc. student in Computer Science at ETH Zurich. My research goal is to build interpretable models that reveal how genetic variation and perturbations propagate through context-specific molecular networks to cause disease. I approach this from two directions:

- **Modeling biological networks:** context-aware, multimodal graph models that capture how genetic perturbations spread through molecular networks.
- **Interpretability of biomedical ML:** methods that uncover what these models learn and test whether it yields reliable biological hypotheses.

Currently, I am a Visiting Graduate Researcher in the [Martin Zhang Lab](https://mzhanglab.github.io/) at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Martin Jinye Zhang and Prof. Michelle Li, where I work to enhance KGWAS, a knowledge graph-based model for genome-wide association studies. At ETH Zurich, I worked with Prof. Gunnar Rätsch in the [Biomedical Informatics Group](https://bmi.inf.ethz.ch/) on training sparse autoencoders to interpret and steer clinical time-series foundation models.

Previously, I received a B.Sc. in Mathematics (Honors) and a B.Sc. in Computer Science (Departmental Honors) from the University of Texas at Austin, where I worked with [Prof. Can Cenik](https://ceniklab.github.io/) on translation efficiency covariation networks. I also spent a summer at the [Physiological Laboratories](https://phyzlab.org/) at Dell Medical School with [Prof. Joshua Chang](https://jtkchang.github.io/), evaluating deep learning models for heart rate forecasting in preterm infants.
